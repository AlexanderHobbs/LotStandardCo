Accessible parking isn't optional, and getting it wrong can mean fines, complaints, or worse — a visitor who can't safely access your building. Here's what property managers need to know.

<!--more-->

## How many accessible spaces do you need?

The ADA sets minimum accessible-space counts based on total lot size, on a sliding scale — a 25-space lot needs at least 1 accessible space, while larger lots require proportionally more, including a percentage of van-accessible spaces.

## What "compliant" actually means

- Accessible spaces must be the correct width, with an adjacent access aisle
- Van-accessible spaces need a wider aisle than standard accessible spaces
- Signage must be mounted at the correct height and be clearly visible
- The route from the space to the building entrance must be an accessible path, not just a striped space

## A common mistake

Faded or incorrectly-placed accessible markings are one of the most common compliance gaps we see — often because the original layout was correct, but repeated re-striping over the years slowly drifted from code as lines got repainted freehand.
