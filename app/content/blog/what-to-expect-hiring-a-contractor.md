If you've never had a lot professionally striped before, it helps to know what the process looks like from quote to completion. A good contractor makes it straightforward, and knowing the steps makes it easier to compare quotes and spot problems. Here's how a typical project goes.

<!--more-->

## 1. Site visit & quote

We start with a walk-through of your property to measure the lot, note any ADA or fire-lane requirements, and understand your traffic patterns. We'll ask about what's bothering you, whether it's faded stalls, a changed layout, a new tenant or a compliance concern. You'll get a written quote with no surprise add-ons.

A good quote should spell out:

- What's being painted (stalls, fire lanes, arrows, accessible spaces, stop bars, numbering and so on)
- How many stalls and what kind of paint
- Any prep work, such as cleaning or removing old lines
- The price and what it covers
- When the work can be scheduled

If a quote is vague, ask for details. If it's far lower than the others, find out what's missing.

## 2. Scheduling

We work around your business hours whenever possible — early mornings, evenings, or weekends — to minimize disruption. For larger lots we can break the work into sections so part of the lot stays open.

Let us know about busy periods, events, deliveries and anything else we should plan around. Weather matters too, since paint needs dry conditions, so we'll confirm the date close to the day and have a backup in mind.

## 3. Surface prep

Before painting, we sweep and clean the surface so paint adheres properly and lasts as long as it should. If the old lines are being changed, we'll mark out the new layout and, where needed, cover or remove the old markings so drivers aren't confused by ghost lines.

Preparation is the part customers don't see, and it's one of the biggest factors in how long the job lasts.

## 4. Layout & striping

We lay out stalls, fire lanes, arrows, and ADA-compliant accessible spaces according to code, then apply fast-drying traffic paint. Measuring the layout first keeps stalls straight and consistent, and lets us check that accessible spaces, access aisles and fire lanes meet the dimensions they should.

## 5. Cure time & walkthrough

Most paint is dry to the touch within an hour, with full cure happening over the following day. Cones and barriers keep traffic off the fresh lines until they're ready. We do a final walkthrough with you to make sure everything meets expectations, and fix anything that doesn't.

## What we need from you

- Access to the lot at the agreed time, and vehicles moved out of the way where possible
- A heads-up to tenants, staff and customers about the schedule
- Any known details such as accessible-space requirements, fire marshal notes or HOA rules
- Someone available for the final walkthrough

## Questions to ask any striping contractor

1. Are you licensed and insured, and can I see proof?
2. Who will be on site, and who is my point of contact?
3. What paint do you use, and how long should it last?
4. How do you handle ADA and fire-lane requirements?
5. What happens if the weather doesn't cooperate?
6. Is the quote written, and what does it include?
7. Do you document the finished work?

## How long does it take?

Most commercial lots take a day or less, depending on size, layout complexity and the amount of prep needed. A small lot can often be finished in a few hours; a large retail or apartment lot may be done in phases.
