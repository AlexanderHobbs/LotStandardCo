Sealcoating is one of the most cost-effective ways to protect your pavement investment, but timing matters. Apply it too early and you risk wasting money on a surface that doesn't need it yet; wait too long and you lose the protection you paid for. Here's how to decide when your lot is due.

<!--more-->

## The short answer

Most commercial lots should be sealcoated every 2–3 years, depending on traffic volume, climate, and the age of the asphalt. High-traffic lots (grocery stores, shopping centers) tend toward the shorter end of that range, while low-traffic lots (offices, small retail) can often stretch closer to 3 years.

New asphalt is usually left to cure for a period before its first seal coat, so ask your paving contractor how long to wait.

## What affects the timing

- **Traffic volume and weight.** Constant turning movements, delivery trucks and trash trucks wear a surface faster than light passenger-car traffic.
- **Sun exposure.** UV light breaks down the asphalt binder, so lots with little shade fade and oxidize sooner.
- **Climate.** Heat, heavy rain and freeze-thaw cycles all work on a surface, and moisture that seeps into small cracks can expand when it freezes.
- **Oil and fuel drips.** Parking stalls and drive-through lanes take more chemical punishment than other parts of the lot.
- **Drainage.** Areas where water stands wear out faster than areas that drain well.
- **Previous maintenance.** A lot that's had cracks filled and potholes fixed on time holds a seal coat longer.

## Signs your lot needs sealcoating

- The surface looks gray or faded rather than a deep black
- Small surface cracks are starting to appear
- Water isn't beading on the surface anymore
- Loose stone or sand-like aggregate is showing on the surface
- It's been more than 2 years since the last application

If you're seeing larger cracks, sunken areas or alligator-style cracking, the lot may need repairs before sealcoating can do any good. A seal coat protects the surface; it doesn't fix structural damage.

## What happens during sealcoating

A proper job is more than rolling on a black liquid:

1. **Clean the surface.** Dirt, debris and vegetation are removed so the sealer bonds directly to the asphalt.
2. **Treat oil spots.** Stains are primed so the sealer can stick to them.
3. **Fill cracks.** Cracks are sealed before the coating goes on so water can't get beneath the surface.
4. **Apply the sealer.** It's applied evenly, usually in more than one coat on heavily worn areas.
5. **Allow it to cure.** The lot generally needs to stay closed to traffic for a day or two, depending on weather and the product used.
6. **Restripe.** The seal coat covers the old lines, so new striping is the natural last step.

## Why it's worth it

A fresh seal coat isn't just cosmetic — it shields the asphalt binder from UV rays, oil, and water intrusion, all of which accelerate cracking and potholes. It also makes a property look cared for, which matters for tenants and customers. Staying ahead of sealcoating is almost always cheaper than the patch-and-repave cycle that follows neglect.

## Don't sealcoat too often

More isn't always better. Sealing every year builds up thick layers that can crack and flake. Stick to the interval your lot's condition actually calls for, and have it inspected rather than guessing.
