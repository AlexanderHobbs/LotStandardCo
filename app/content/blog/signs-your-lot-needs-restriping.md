Faded lines don't just look bad — they create real safety and liability risk. Drivers park crooked, fire lanes disappear, and accessible spaces become hard to identify. Because paint wears gradually, it's easy to stop noticing it. Here's how to tell when it's time.

<!--more-->

## 5 signs it's time to restripe

1. **You can't tell where one stall ends and the next begins.** If drivers are parking crooked or "eyeballing it," your lines have faded past the point of usefulness. Wasted space also means fewer cars fit in the lot.
2. **Accessible markings are hard to spot.** Faded ADA signage or striping is both a safety issue and a compliance risk. If the symbol or access aisle is hard to see, it's overdue.
3. **Directional arrows and fire lanes are barely visible.** These aren't just for looks — they keep traffic flowing safely and keep emergency access clear. A faded fire lane is hard to enforce.
4. **It's been more than 18-24 months since the last striping job.** Even in mild climates, traffic paint wears down steadily, and busy lots wear faster.
5. **You've had new sealcoating applied.** Fresh sealcoat covers your old lines completely — re-striping is the natural next step.

## Why faded striping matters

**Safety.** Clear lines tell drivers where to go and where to stop. Without them you get more fender benders, near-misses with pedestrians, and blocked lanes.

**Liability.** If someone is hurt in a lot with worn markings, the condition of the paint can come up later. Keeping your lot clearly marked is a simple way to show you maintain the property.

**Compliance.** Accessible spaces, access aisles and fire lanes may be required by code. A lot that was compliant when it was painted may not look compliant now.

**Appearance.** Customers, tenants and visitors form an impression of your property before they walk through the door. Crisp lines tell them the property is cared for.

**Capacity.** Clear stalls let people park straight and use every space. Lots with faded lines tend to lose parking spaces to sloppy parking.

## Other things to look for

- Lines that are chipped or peeling instead of just faded
- Stop bars and crosswalks that no longer stand out
- Stall numbers or reserved-parking markings that can't be read
- Curbs where the paint has worn away
- Places where the old layout no longer fits how the lot is used

## How long striping lasts

There's no single answer. Paint on a busy retail lot may show wear in a year, while a lightly used office lot can look good for much longer. Traffic, weather, the surface, the paint and how well the lines were prepared all play a part. If you're not sure, look at the lines in the busiest areas — the entrance, the aisle in front of the main door and the turning lanes — since they wear first.

## What happens when you restripe

A good restriping job is more than painting over the old lines:

1. **Walk the lot** and check the layout against the existing lines, code requirements and how the lot is actually used.
2. **Clean the surface** so the paint can bond.
3. **Mark and measure** the layout so the stalls are straight and consistent.
4. **Paint** stalls, arrows, fire lanes, accessible spaces and any other markings.
5. **Let it dry** and then reopen the lot. Most paint is dry to the touch within a short time.

If the lot is also due for sealcoating or crack repair, it's most efficient to do those first and stripe last, so you only close the lot once.
